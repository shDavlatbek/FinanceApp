Tally

Tally is a personal money tracking application for Android and iOS. It records what you earn, what you spend and what you move between your own accounts, and shows you where your money went.

It runs on your phone and works with no internet connection. Connecting your own Google Drive is optional, and lets Tally keep two of your own devices showing the same records. There is no Tally server, no account to create and nothing to sign up for.

What Tally does

  • Records transactions — an amount, a category, the account the money moved through, an optional note, and when it happened.
  • Tracks accounts — cash, cards, savings and investment pots, each with its own running balance.
  • Moves money between them without counting it as spending, so putting money aside does not read as losing it.
  • Summarises a day, a month or a custom span: income, spending, a breakdown by category, and trends over time.
  • Accepts entries from a Telegram bot you run yourself, so logging an expense is one short message rather than opening an app.
  • Exports and imports your data as a JSON backup or a CSV spreadsheet, so you can leave at any time.
  • Works entirely offline. Connecting Google Drive is optional; without it Tally is still a complete tracker.

Why Tally asks for access to Google Drive

Tally has no servers of its own, so it needs somewhere neutral to leave a file when your phone and your Telegram bot need to agree on what you have recorded. It uses your Google Drive for exactly that, and for nothing else.

It requests a single Google permission:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file

“See, edit, create, and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app.”

Here is precisely what Tally does with it:

  1. Creates one folder in your Drive, named Tally.
  2. Writes one small JSON file inside it per device — tally-<device-id>.json — containing that device's copy of your transactions, categories, accounts and settings.
  3. Reads the file your other device wrote, and merges the two so both ends show the same records.

That is the whole use. Tally does not read, list or modify any other file in your Drive — the drive.file permission does not allow it to, and Google enforces that, not us. It does not request access to your email, your contacts, your calendar, your profile or any other Google service.

Tally's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where your data lives

On your phone, on the server you run, and — only if you connect it — in the Tally folder of your own Google Drive, as readable JSON you can open yourself. The people who wrote Tally operate no service, receive no copy of your records and cannot see them. There is nothing to sign up for and no account to create.

Full detail in the privacy policy, including how to revoke Tally's access to Drive and delete everything it has stored.

Getting Tally

Tally is open-source software under the MIT licence. You install the app on your phone and, if you want the Telegram bot and syncing, run the server half yourself — one container that only ever makes outbound calls, so it needs no public hostname and no certificate.
