Privacy
Policy

Tally is software you run yourself. It has no accounts, no analytics and no backend of ours. This page explains what it stores, where that lives, and what the Google permission it asks for actually allows.

The short version

  • We collect nothing. There is no Tally-operated server. No data about you reaches us, because there is nowhere for it to go.
  • Your data lives on your device and in your own Google Drive, in a folder you can open, read and delete yourself.
  • Tally can only touch the files it created. The one Google permission it requests, drive.file, gives it no access to anything else in your Drive.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no profiling, and nothing is ever sold or shared.

Who is responsible

Tally is open-source software distributed under the MIT licence. You install the app and run the server yourself, using your own Google account, your own Telegram bot and your own hardware. In data-protection terms you are the controller of your own data; the authors of Tally are not, because they never receive it.

This site exists to publish this policy and the terms, and to satisfy Google's requirement that an application using OAuth links to both. The site itself sets no cookies, runs no scripts and loads nothing from a third party — the fonts it uses are served from this domain, not from a font service.

What Tally stores, and where

On your phone
Your transactions (amount, kind, category, account, optional note, date and time), your categories and accounts, and your settings — currency, language, the account the bot books to. This lives in a local database on the device and never leaves it unless you connect Drive.
On the server you run
The same records, in a SQLite file on your own machine, plus the Telegram user IDs you chose to allow and the shorthand the bot has learned from your messages.
In your Google Drive, only if you connect it
One folder, Tally, containing one JSON file per device (tally-<device-id>.json). Each file is a full copy of that device's records. They are plain, readable JSON on purpose: you can open them, and they double as your backup.
Credentials
The Google refresh token obtained when you connect Drive is stored locally — in the app's own database on the phone, and in a file readable only by the owner (mode 0600) on the server. It is never transmitted anywhere except to Google when refreshing access.

Tally works with no Drive connected at all. Sync is optional, and turning it off is a single button in Settings.

Google user data

Tally requests exactly one OAuth scope:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file

“See, edit, create, and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app.”

This scope is limited by Google to files the application itself created. It is used for one purpose only: to write and read the snapshot files described above, so that the phone and your server can exchange transactions without a server of ours in the middle. Tally does not request access to your other files, your email, your contacts, your profile or any other Google service.

Tally's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Concretely, that means data obtained through the Drive API is used only to provide sync inside your own installation; it is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising, not used to train any model, and not read by a human.

Telegram

If you use the Telegram bot, the messages you send it travel through Telegram's infrastructure before reaching the server you run, exactly as any other Telegram message does. That leg is governed by Telegram's own privacy policy, which we do not control. The bot itself stores your Telegram user ID so it can tell you apart from a stranger, and the text of entries you log. Not using the bot removes this entirely — the app is complete on its own.

What Tally does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage statistics.
  • No advertising identifiers and no advertising of any kind.
  • No third-party SDKs that phone home.
  • No selling, renting or sharing of data — there is no data of yours in anyone else's hands to sell.
  • No access to your contacts, photos, location or microphone.

Keeping and deleting your data

Because nothing is held by us, retention is entirely in your hands. Deleting an entry in the app marks it deleted and propagates that deletion to your other device, rather than letting it reappear on the next sync.

To remove everything:

  • Revoke Google access at myaccount.google.com/permissions, or press Disconnect in the app's Settings.
  • Delete the Tally folder in your Google Drive. That removes every snapshot.
  • Uninstall the app to remove the local database, and stop the container and delete its data volume to remove the server's.

You can also simply export a backup first — Settings offers a full JSON backup and a CSV — so that deleting the originals costs you nothing.

Children

Tally is not directed at children and collects nothing from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is published here with a new effective date. Since the software is open source, the behaviour this policy describes can be verified against the source rather than taken on trust.
